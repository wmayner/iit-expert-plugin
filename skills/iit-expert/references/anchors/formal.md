# Style profiles: formal / mathematical group

Six papers, each read in full. All model sentences were checked with `grep -F` and appear verbatim in the cleaned corpus files. Short quoted phrases inside the bullets were checked the same way.

---

## 1. Albantakis et al. 2023, *IIT 4.0* (`albantakis-2023b`)

**Style anchor for:** the canonical overview of the formalism, including the axiom-to-postulate translation, how the postulates are applied step by step (maximal cause–effect state → φs → complex → distinctions/relations → Φ-structure), the ontological principles, and the "big picture" implications (connectivity, activity vs. inactivation, being vs. doing).

**Voice profile**
- **Each step is anchored to a postulate or principle.** Almost every new step opens by naming the requirement that motivates it. Examples: "By the integration postulate, …", "By the exclusion postulate, …", "Once again, we employ the principle of maximal existence …", "In line with existence, intrinsicality, and information, …". Subsections are run-in headers named after the postulates ("Existence. According to IIT, existence understood as cause–effect power requires …"). The math comes second, as the way a requirement is carried out.
- **Definitions move from requirement to quantity to name.** The paper states what must be true and says how it is assessed ("This can be assessed by partitioning …"). Only then does it name the quantity, often in parentheses with a nickname ("structure integrated information Φ ('big Phi,' 'structure Phi')"). Each definition ends with a one-line verbal gloss ("φs … is thus a quantifier of irreducible existence").
- **Every formal statement is also said in words.** Equations are followed by "In words, …" or "In other words, …", and set-theoretic conditions are spelled out plainly ("each part must contain at least one unit, there must be no overlap …"). Summary sentences use "Altogether," "Taken together," and "In summary,".
- **"We" for procedure, "IIT" as the agent of claims, and "I/my" for phenomenology.** "IIT proposes …", "IIT identifies …", "According to IIT, …". The first-person singular appears only in the axiom examples ("if I awaken from dreamless sleep and experience the white wall of my room …").
- **Examples are worked from toy networks to the brain.** Each Results subsection describes a small network, reports the complex and Φ, and then makes a measured bridge to neuroanatomy with markers like "Notably," "Intriguingly," and "A similar situation may occur in the brain." Each ends with a crisp moral.
- **Hedging is confident and principled.** "In principle" is frequent, and "must still be considered as work in progress" appears. The conclusion admits limits openly ("Even so, there are many ways in which IIT may turn out to be inadequate or wrong."). Hedges sit on feasibility and empirical status, never on the axioms.
- **Rhythm:** long, dense expository paragraphs (150–300 words) broken by short pivot sentences and rhetorical questions ("But which one?").

**Model sentences**
1. "By the exclusion postulate, the substrate of consciousness must be definite; that is, it must comprise a definite set of units." (Exclusion: Determining maximal substrates)
2. "In other words, what a single unit contributes to intrinsic existence is not some small “bit” of information." (Consciousness and activity)
3. "The physical correspondent of an experience is not the substrate as such but the Φ-structure specified by the substrate in its current state." (Conclusions)

**Don't imitate**
- Numbered equation blocks, pointers such as "(see (8) in S1 Notes)", "S2 Text", and "Fig 6C", and bracketed citation runs.
- Tuple and set notation for states (u = {(Ui, state(Ui))}), the lowercase/uppercase state-label convention (e.g., "Abcdef"), and figure-caption prose about colored edges and faces.

## 2. Marshall et al. 2023, *System Integrated Information* (`marshall-2023`)

**Style anchor for:** how φs is defined and computed (intrinsic information, directional partitions, the minimum partition and its normalization, identifying complexes), and how determinism, degeneracy, and fault lines affect φs.

**Voice profile**
- **Sections are the postulates, in order.** Sections 2.1–2.4 are titled Intrinsicality, Information, Integration, and Exclusion. Each opens by restating the postulate ("The integration postulate states that …"), then turns it into a procedural question ("To evaluate irreducibility, we ask whether …"), then an operation ("To do so, we cut the system …").
- **Principles are used as the justification step.** "IIT appeals to the principle of maximal existence", then the principle is stated in one sentence, then "Accordingly, …" introduces the definition. The rhythm is postulate → principle → "Accordingly" → definition.
- **Definitions are compact and use "we".** Phrases include "we define", "is defined as", and "we identify a complex by finding …". Each term is glossed right away in plain language: the minimum partition is "the 'weakest link' or fault line of the system".
- **Examples are narrated in the past tense, like an experiment.** "First, we considered …", "To highlight the impact of degeneracy, we considered a modified system where …". Each example ends by generalizing ("In general, higher values of intrinsic information allow for higher values of integrated information.").
- **Connectives are plain and logical:** "Thus," "Accordingly," "However," "Moreover," "By contrast," "In general,". There is little rhetorical flourish.
- **Hedging is limited to scope.** "For simplicity, in this work we restrict consideration to …" and "While IIT must still be considered a work in progress, …". Claims about the measure itself are stated flatly.
- **Rhythm:** medium-length paragraphs, each with one job (state, justify, define, remark). Short rhetorical pivots appear ("The question is, which set?").

**Model sentences**
1. "For a system to exist intrinsically, it must have cause–effect power within itself." (2.1 Intrinsicality)
2. "By the principle of minimal existence, a system cannot exist as one system more than it exists across its weakest link." (2.3 Integration)
3. "However, for defining a complex, it is not the absolute value of ϕs that matters, only whether or not it is maximal." (3.3 Example 3: Exclusion)

**Don't imitate**
- Numeric result reporting (e.g., integrated information as a percentage of intrinsic information, connection weights like w = 0.45), "Figure 2B" references, and "see Appendix C".
- Theorem/proof formatting and algorithm pseudocode.

## 3. Marshall, Findlay, Albantakis & Tononi 2024, *Intrinsic Units* (`marshall-2026`)

**Style anchor for:** the grain of units and macro, meso, and micro units; "maximally irreducible within" vs. "within and without"; why units are binary; background conditions at macro grains; and what IIT predicts about which neural changes affect experience.

**Voice profile**
- **The introduction is driven by questions.** It opens with open empirical questions ("Are the units individual neurons, synapses, groups of neurons, or …?") before bringing in IIT. The same pattern recurs as "Consider the requirement for integration." / "Consider also the requirement for exclusion.", with each postulate applied in turn to units.
- **Arguments proceed by analogy and contrast with complexes:** "Just like a complex, …", "Just as complexes must be definite, so must macro units: …", "However, unlike complexes, intrinsic units only need to be …". Each section closes with a summary line: "In summary, an intrinsic unit must be …".
- **Formal material is previewed in words first.** An explicit "conceptual overview" subsection ("Here, we describe the process at a high level with some intuition for each step.") comes before the full mathematics. Signposts include "Put simply," "Roughly speaking," and "We now consider the requirements … more formally."
- **Reductio phrasing guards the requirements.** Figure titles include "Out of nothing, nothing comes", and the text says "building something … out of nothing" and "There is nothing unitary about it, except possibly from the extrinsic perspective of an experimenter." There is also a steady intrinsic/extrinsic contrast ("Of course, from the extrinsic perspective of an experimenter …").
- **"We" for the framework; "IIT predicts" for consequences.** The Discussion moves from formal results to concrete neural claims (update grain of about 30 ms; changes in firing timing that do not change the macro state do not affect experience).
- **Hedging is explicit about idealization.** "In practice, the determination of complexes … cannot be based on a full knowledge of the micro-physical TPM." There is also "We conjecture that …" and "Stationarity … is also merely a convenient assumption."
- **Rhythm:** long, carefully built paragraphs with enumerated conditions ("(1) … (2) … (3) …") and short, emphatic closers.

**Model sentences**
1. "Just as complexes must be definite, so must macro units: there must be a reason why a unit has the border it has." (2.2.2 Applying the postulates to units)
2. "Thus, there is always a reason why a complex and its intrinsic units are precisely what they are: the principle of maximal existence." (2.2.2, end)
3. "Accordingly, IIT predicts that experience should only change if there is a change in the state of intrinsic units at their intrinsic update grain." (Discussion)

**Don't imitate**
- Heavy notation (J = (U^J, V^J, τ′J, g′J, W^J), prescripts like ₜů, and state-to-sequence probabilities) and step-by-step "Step 1–4" algorithm prose with "Eqn. 28" references.
- Footnoted historical asides about earlier versions of the theory, and mapping counts like "2∧(2∧(τ′|V|) − 1) − 1".

## 4. Mayner, Marshall & Tononi 2026, *Intrinsic Cause–Effect Power* (`mayner-2026`)

**Style anchor for:** intrinsic differentiation vs. intrinsic specification, the determinism/indeterminism tradeoff, why a perfectly deterministic system has no intrinsic alternatives, monads, and links to criticality, empowerment, and free will ("true alternatives").

**Voice profile**
- **The argument starts from intuition, then formalizes it.** The Theory section opens with a thesis sentence, then a thought experiment ("To illustrate this point, consider the case of a single-unit system …" with a COPY gate seen by an experimenter versus "for the system itself"), then "This section formalizes the above intuition by (i) … and (ii) …".
- **Desiderata come before the definition:** "The measure should be similar to entropy, in that … Moreover, the units … should be … With these considerations in mind, we define …". This is a clean model for introducing a new quantity.
- **Distinctions are clarified by contrast.** "It is important to distinguish X from Y per se." and "It is also worth clarifying the relationship between X and entropy." The pattern is "X is not a Y: Y is …, whereas X is …".
- **Requirements are stated in pairs.** "two complementary requirements. First, … Second, …", "both (i) … and (ii) …", "dual requirements". Principles are cited as the reason for taking a minimum ("Again, by the principle of minimal existence, …").
- **Each section ends with a recap line:** "Altogether, φs (s) quantifies …", and the examples section opens by restating what Section 2 did. The Discussion begins "In this work, we refined …" and then states the "central motivation".
- **The Discussion is outward-looking and modestly hedged.** It says "resonates with", "remains subject to debate", and "an important direction for future work", and contrasts carefully with neighboring notions ("However, empowerment is an extrinsic, observer-defined quantity, whereas …").
- **Rhythm:** short-to-medium paragraphs and very clear topic sentences. Of the group, this is the easiest to lift into a chat answer.

**Model sentences**
1. "A physical system can possess intrinsic cause–effect power only to the extent that it can provide itself with a repertoire of potential states." (2. Theory, opening)
2. "It is important to distinguish intrinsic differentiation from indeterminism per se." (2.2 Intrinsic Differentiation)
3. "The present framework emphasizes that differentiation is not a nuisance to be minimized, but a necessary ingredient for intrinsic existence." (4. Discussion)

**Don't imitate**
- Piecewise equation displays (− log(p) if s̄ = 0 …), numerical optima such as "p = 0.744, where φs = 0.427", and "(Figure 3F)" references.
- Stacked sub- and superscripts (ic/e diff, p←c) written inline in prose.

## 5. Albantakis & Tononi 2019, *Causal Composition* (`albantakis-2019a`)

**Style anchor for:** composition as a formal point, including why the intrinsic information of a system requires evaluating all subsets (reductionist vs. holistic vs. compositional perspectives), the difference from partial information decomposition (PID) and information decomposition in general, dynamically equivalent systems that differ causally, and agent/environment borders.

**Voice profile**
- **The framing is a three-way contrast of perspectives:** reductionist vs. holistic vs. compositional. Most arguments place a claim on this triad ("Taking a reductionist perspective, … Taking a holistic perspective, … Nevertheless, only a compositional analysis …").
- **Explicit versus implicit information is a core move.** Short aphoristic sentences mark the turn ("However, information that has to be inferred remains implicit."), followed by the intrinsic-perspective conclusion.
- **The paper speaks as a bridge to standard information theory.** It starts from entropy, mutual information, and effective information, and then shows what they miss. It deliberately simplifies ("Here, however, we want to remain as close as possible to standard measures of information theory …"). "In our view," appears once. This is less postulate-anchored than IIT 4.0, with "intrinsicality", "composition", and "integration" used as run-in paragraph labels.
- **Worked readings of a single example system.** The text walks through what each subset "specifies" about another, in the present tense ("Ct = 1, for example, only specifies that Mt+1 is more likely to be on …").
- **Enumerated comparisons with other frameworks:** "Our approach differs from PID measures in several ways. First, … Second, … Finally, …".
- **Discussion questions and moral closers.** "How should the environment be incorporated …? And how can we identify the agent …?" Arguments end with pointed lines ("far from ontologically innocent"). Hedging is scoped: "should hold even in the case of an infinite state space", "may serve as practical indicators".
- **Rhythm:** long paragraphs in the methods, and medium paragraphs in the results and discussion, each ending in a one-sentence takeaway.

**Model sentences**
1. "However, information that has to be inferred remains implicit." (2.1 The Compositional Intrinsic Information of an Example System)
2. "From the intrinsic perspective of the system, information about its causes and effects is thus only available if it is made explicit by some mechanism within the system." (2.1)
3. "This shows that global dynamical equivalence does not imply physical equivalence in a more general sense." (3.2 Global vs. Physical Reversibility)

**Don't imitate**
- Outdated measure names and machinery (Φ⊆, DKL-based φ, MIP, "purview" tables with bit values). In a chat answer these would suggest a superseded formalism. Borrow the argumentative shape, not the measures.
- Tables of φ values per subset, PyPhi config settings, and Spearman correlations.

## 6. Zaeemzadeh & Tononi 2024, *Shannon Information and Integrated Information: Message and Meaning* (`zaeemzadeh-2024a`)

**Style anchor for:** Shannon information vs. integrated information; meaning ("the feeling is the meaning"); codes, decoding, and "information processing" in neuroscience; the communication of meaning between subjects; and why computers or the cerebellum "signify nothing" intrinsically.

**Voice profile**
- **It opens with a narrative scenario and returns to it.** Alice, Bob, and the house on fire open the paper, and later sections come back to them ("let us now return to Alice and Bob"). The intro ends with a two-part question ("But how does information convey meaning, and where is the meaning to be found?").
- **Postulates are used as a parallel contrast list.** Each item has a label ("(3rd) Segregated vs. integrated."), then a two-clause thesis joined by a semicolon ("Shannon information …; integrated information requires …"), then a figure-backed illustration, then "By IIT's X postulate, however, …".
- **Summary formulas are frequent:** "In short, …" (many times), "the feeling is the meaning", and "information is a structure, not a symbol or code". There are memorable antitheses (message/meaning, extrinsic/intrinsic, code/structure).
- **The register is essayistic and addressed to the reader.** It uses "let us", "Consider …", "Just imagine a world devoid of conscious beings …", and "Or consider the cerebellum." The first-person plural is used lightly. Evidence (cerebellum, blood pressure regulation "in the dark") is used as vivid, concrete illustration rather than data.
- **It is fair to the rival view before turning.** "The information processing approach … has been successful in explaining many aspects … However, its shortcomings should not be overlooked." A run of rhetorical questions follows ("Should one decode …? Based on what criteria?").
- **Technical detail is pushed to footnotes and supplements.** The main text stays conceptual, and the supplements carry the textbook detail. Hedges are mild ("In principle," "per IIT", "If IIT is right, however, …").
- **Rhythm:** varied paragraph length, short punchy sentences mixed with long enumerations, and a conversational tempo. This is the most chat-transferable paper in the group.

**Model sentences**
1. "But how does information convey meaning, and where is the meaning to be found?" (Introduction)
2. "Shannon information benefits from segregation; integrated information requires the substrate’s irreducibility." (Shannon information and integrated information side-by-side, (3rd) Segregated vs. integrated)
3. "In short, information is a structure, not a symbol or code, a process, a computation, or a function." (Information processing vs. information structures)

**Don't imitate**
- Figure-panel walkthroughs ("In Fig. 6(a) …", "Fig. 4(d) shows how …"), numbered footnote asides, and "Supplementary material I" pointers.
- The Alice/Bob scenario as a reusable template in every answer. Use a fresh concrete case rather than copying the paper's.

## Group summary

All six make the postulates and the principles of maximal/minimal existence the grammar of the argument: requirement → "by the X postulate/principle" → "accordingly" → definition → plain-language gloss → toy example. All use "we" for procedure and "IIT" as the agent of claims, contrast intrinsic with extrinsic, and hedge only on feasibility and idealization, never on the axioms.

They differ in register:
- **IIT 4.0:** authoritative reference, broadest and most confident, from phenomenology to brain.
- **Marshall 2023:** leanest, postulate-by-postulate, past-tense examples, almost no rhetoric.
- **Marshall 2024:** argues by analogy ("just as complexes … so must units") and states predictions.
- **Mayner 2026:** cleanest template for a new quantity (intuition → desiderata → definition).
- **Albantakis 2019:** talks to standard information theory through the reductionist/holistic/compositional triad.
- **Zaeemzadeh 2024:** essayistic, scenario-driven, "In short" antitheses; the easiest to carry into chat.
