# IIT prose-style profiles: ontology / philosophy / debate / empirical group

Scope: prose style only (not content). All seven papers were read in full; references, tables of DOIs, and PDF-extraction noise were ignored. Tononi et al. 2024 was read from the IIT Expert MCP (slug `tononi-2024`). All model sentences were checked by exact string match against the source text. Where a source has an inline citation or footnote marker, it is kept as printed.

## 1. Tononi, Albantakis, Boly, Cirelli & Koch 2022, "Only what exists can cause: An intrinsic view of free will" (MCP `tononi-2023`)

**Style anchor for:** free will, agency, responsibility, determinism and predictability, "do I exist or do my neurons", intrinsic vs. extrinsic existence and causation, the intrinsic powers ontology, emergence, and causal exclusion.

**Voice profile**
- **First-person-singular thesis voice.** The authors write as "I", with "we" reserved for the argument. The claim is put in the first person, contrasted with neurons, and made emphatic by repetition: "So it is I who exist—not my neurons." Section titles use the same frame ("I have alternatives—not my neurons"). The word "true" is repeated as a scalpel: true alternatives, true decisions, true responsibility.
- **Openings.** Sections open either with a plain, naive question ("What could be easier than raising my hand if I decide to do so?") or with a recap of where the argument stands ("Up to now, we have addressed the question 'what exists?' ... We now turn to..."). The essay announces its structure early (sec. 2, sec. 3...) and later sends the reader back to it: "Recall the conclusion from section 3.5... And recall the conclusion from section 4.5..."
- **Opposing view stated fairly, then taken apart by its own logic.** The rival ("extrinsic substrate+ ontologies") is named, bundled, and given its best reasons ("These emergent properties and constructs are considered 'real' for two main reasons. First... Second..."). It is then followed to an uncomfortable end ("I am just 'carried along for the ride'"), which the text labels a "cognitive dissonance" that is "resolved only by abandoning" the view. Each rebuttal ends by repeating the thesis in a new form.
- **Ontological claims as definitions and contrasts.** The authors assert by definition, by explanatory identity, and by named principles (principle of maximal existence, principle of no free existence, "great divide of being"). Short equations of distinction appear: "existence is not constitution", "prediction is not the same as causation". Colon constructions do the defining ("to exist in physical terms means to have cause–effect power—being able to take and make a difference").
- **Signposting and connectives.** Frequent: "In other words", "Said otherwise", "In short", "Thus", "Moreover", "By contrast", "Instead", "As we have seen", "As we will now argue". Em-dashes appositively gloss a term right after it is used ("an intrinsic entity—one that exists intrinsically, for itself").
- **Thought experiments and analogies are concrete and domestic.** Examples include raising a hand, driving on autopilot, voting on a resolution, a rock hitting an empty car, two parallel domino chains, a world where everyone is in dreamless sleep, and the "fork in the mind (Y)" vs. "fork in the road (-<)". Each is introduced with "Consider" or "Say I...", run through quickly, and then interpreted.
- **Empirical material is brief and conditional.** Evidence is subordinate to the argument and hedged with "likely", "presumably" and "Ongoing work using approximate measures suggests". Tests are framed as questions (numbered predictions). Paragraphs are long and layered, with a crisp one-line restatement at the end.

**Model sentences**
1. "In other words, there cannot be free will without consciousness, but there can certainly be consciousness without free will." (§5 Requirements for free will)
2. "In such a world of ontological dust, where nothing would feel like anything much, nothing would exist for which it would be true that something exists." (§3.3 Intrinsic entities, extrinsic entities, and the great divide of being)
3. "This dissonance is resolved only by abandoning the extrinsic substrate+ view and recognizing the intrinsic powers view as the proper ontological starting point (tables III and IV)." (§7.1 No free will? Ontological and causal micro-determination)

**Don't imitate**
- The sustained first-person "I/my neurons" ventriloquism and long chains of "true X" repeated over many paragraphs. In chat, use it once, for emphasis, rather than as the running register.
- Literary epigraphs, quotations from sages and the Frank Outlaw anecdote, and cross-references by section number ("recall section 3.5").

## 2. Grasso, Albantakis, Lang & Tononi 2021, "Causal reductionism and causal structures" (Nature Neuroscience Perspective; not in the IIT Expert library)

**Style anchor for:** causal reductionism, "a pack of neurons", macro vs. micro causation, causation vs. prediction vs. supervenience, high-order mechanisms, distributed representations, actual causation ("what caused what?"), and composition and irreducibility in causal analysis.

**Voice profile**
- **Open with the intuition you are going to overturn.** The paper opens by granting the consensus ("Everybody understands that an organism is more than the sum of its parts"), pivots on "Yet almost everybody also seems to believe...", and gives the intuition its most forceful spokesman (the Crick quote) before announcing the verdict in one flat sentence.
- **Toy model as the whole argument.** A graded series of simulated organisms (F3, F2, F1 frogs) is introduced like a lab report ("we must open their head and study their brain as neuroscientists would do"). The reductionist reading is applied to each in turn until it "now seems absurd". The final contrast is then stated in a summary sentence ("In sum, causal reductionism is blind to...").
- **Diagnose the rival, don't just reject it.** The opposing view is credited with what it gets right ("rightly recognizes the importance of causal irreducibility") and then shown to be "implicit and intuitive rather than explicit and operational". Key moves rest on the paired terms "horizontal determination" (prediction) and "vertical determination" (supervenience).
- **Measured "we" and restrained assertion.** The authors write "We think...", "we argue", "What is needed, we believe, is...". Claims about causes are backed by explicit operations (perturbations, partitions, α > 0 vs. α = 0), with no metaphysical language beyond what the example requires.
- **Connectives.** "However", "Moreover", "Indeed", "In other words", "Thus", "In sum", "Not so, however, with...", "To drive the point home from a different angle". The authors also use rhetorical self-questions: "But how does causal reductionism treat F2 frogs?"
- **Hedging and rhythm.** The tone is tentative only at the pivot ("Perhaps something is not right, then, with the way causal reductionism conceives of causation."). Elsewhere the prose is confident and compact, with medium-length paragraphs. Technical detail (probabilities, states such as SLSCSR = 101) goes in parentheses so that the main sentence still reads cleanly.

**Model sentences**
1. "We think that this deep-seated, widespread intuition of causal reductionism is fundamentally wrong." (Introduction)
2. "Indeed, once the state of each individual neuron is accounted for, there is nothing left to predict, but, as we will see, there can be plenty left to be caused." (Introduction)
3. "The essential point made by these simple examples is this: causal reductionism rightly recognizes the importance of causal irreducibility, but it does so implicitly and intuitively rather than explicitly and operationally." (A simple example: causal reductionism, after F1 frogs)

**Don't imitate**
- Dense state-notation strings (CLCCCR = 101, p = 0.8) inline in prose. In chat, name the mechanism in words and give at most one value.
- The multi-box Nature layout (Box 1–3 carrying half the argument). A chat answer should put its argument in the main text.

## 3. Ellia, Hendren, Grasso, Kozma, Mindt, Lang, Haun, Albantakis, Boly & Tononi 2021, "Consciousness and the fallacy of misplaced objectivity" (MCP `ellia-2021`)

**Style anchor for:** functionalism and access vs. phenomenal consciousness, "consciousness is what consciousness does", report paradigms and rich vs. sparse experience, whether science can explain subjective properties, the hard problem framed methodologically, introspection as evidence, and inference to a good explanation.

**Voice profile**
- **Name the fallacy, then defend against it.** The paper coins a label (modelled on Whitehead) and defines it in one colon sentence. The label is then reused at every turn ("This view, we argue, commits the fallacy of misplaced objectivity"). Headings are rhetorical questions or chiasmi: "Consciousness is what consciousness does?", "Do I say what I see, or do I see what I say?", "Science or magic?".
- **Historical walk-through as setup.** Behaviorism, then cognitive functionalism, then NCC research. Each is given a fair paragraph with a representative quote (Watson, Cohen & Dennett, Crick & Koch), credited for its success, and then shown to leave the phenomenon out.
- **Aphoristic inversions and chiasmus.** Examples: "I am seeing it, so I can say it" vs. "I say it, so I must be seeing it"; "puts the cart before the horse, or, more elegantly, inverts the epistemic order"; "what it is, not by what it does"; "consciousness is about being—not just about doing". Idioms are used knowingly ("the proof of the epistemic pudding").
- **Mixed person, pedagogically deployed.** The authors use "we argue" for the thesis, "you" for the experimental walkthrough ("Suppose you are an experimental subject looking at a large, dark screen"), and "I" for introspective reasoning ("how could I see the spots located where they are if I did not see how far apart they are?"). Cascades of rhetorical questions build to a collapse ("the distance would collapse; all distances would collapse; the very canvas ... would collapse").
- **Stakes framed as magic vs. physics.** The recurring threat is that the way experience feels would be "consigned to magic rather than science". "There must be a reason why..." is repeated as a premise.
- **Empirical reporting as support, not centerpiece.** Clinical and lab findings (Glasgow Coma Scale, anesthesia awareness, hemianopia, white dreams) are listed briskly, each with a "For example" or "Conversely". Hedging is light ("we now think that", "it seems that the first hypothesis will turn out to be better"). Paragraphs run medium to long, with short punchy closers ("In a word, everything." / "It should be done, and it can be done.").

**Model sentences**
1. "Put differently, “I am seeing it, so I can say it” is sensible; “I say it, so I must be seeing it” is absurd." (Science or magic? Banishing experience rather than accounting for it)
2. "Far from ensuring scientific objectivity, it exiles the way experience feels to the realm of magic rather than science." (Science or magic? Banishing experience rather than accounting for it)
3. "It should be clear, then, that an experience is an intrinsic structure, not a function." (The essential properties of every experience: accounting for the presence and absence of consciousness)

**Don't imitate**
- Coining or insisting on a named fallacy and invoking it repeatedly. In a chat answer, one mention is enough; after that, explain.
- Mild condescension ("nearly as silly as denying the existence of a world...", "It is silly because..."). The inversions are useful, but drop the "silly" register.

## 4. Tononi, Albantakis, Barbosa, Boly, ... Zaeemzadeh 2025, "Consciousness or pseudo-consciousness? A clash of two paradigms" (Nature Neuroscience Comment)

**Style anchor for:** answering "Is IIT pseudoscience/untestable?", computational functionalism vs. consciousness-first, AI consciousness ("pseudo-conscious" machines), whether Φ can be computed, whether PCI tests IIT, panpsychism accusations, and the history of IIT's measures. It is the voice for rebuttals of the critics' letter.

**Voice profile**
- **Polemic opening by rhetorical question and epigraph.** The comment opens with a Bacon epigraph and "Understanding consciousness scientifically is hard enough, so why make it harder?". The critics' letter is characterized in a single sardonic line ("much fervor and little fact", "a philosophy-of-science veneer").
- **Reframe the attack as a symptom.** The central move is to turn the criticism into evidence of the rival paradigm's crisis ("these ... heated attacks are symptomatic of a much deeper issue"). Kuhnian vocabulary follows: paradigm, stubborn fact, crisis, threat.
- **Point-by-point rebuttal format.** Box 2 quotes each criticism verbatim and answers it directly, often starting with a verdict word ("This is ironic.", "Again, this is ironic.", "This criticism is incomprehensible, because IIT does just this."). A factual chronology then follows (1994 neural complexity, 1999 effective information, 2008, 2020...).
- **Structured lists in parenthetical imperatives.** For example: "They may be ineffable (try explaining what sadness feels like), defy our analytical skills (try dissecting how Wagner's Tristan chord sounds)...". Parallel lists of dismissals are built from alliterating verbs: "denied, dismissed..., demoted..., discriminated..., or diminished...".
- **Compressed maxims for the AI case.** Examples: "doing without being" / "being without doing"; "'doing vision' is not the same as seeing"; "they will be pseudo-conscious". Analogies are sharp and quick: Leibniz's mill, quantum chemistry and the Schrödinger equation, −272 °C not being "warm", "sulfuric smells to make a case for witchcraft".
- **Hedging where it matters, even in polemic.** Testability is conceded honestly ("may eventually point to a location inconsistent with IIT's predictions"; "of course without 'proving it right'"). Otherwise the tone is assertive and cumulative, with short paragraphs and an ironic historical closer about a "self-appointed tribunal".

**Model sentences**
1. "Paradigms tend to die when a stubborn fact refuses to be accommodated; in this case, the stubborn fact is consciousness itself." (Consciousness and the crisis of the computational-functionalist paradigm)
2. "This criticism is incomprehensible, because IIT does just this." (Box 2: Assorted criticisms and misconceptions)
3. "Functional vision is not the same as phenomenal vision, that is, ‘doing vision’ is not the same as seeing." (Box 1: Is consciousness a structure or a function?)

**Don't imitate**
- Ad hominem and sarcastic register ("corral a hundred academics", "a flock of those who...", "congregation for the doctrine of the faith", witchcraft). Even when a user raises the pseudoscience letter, answer with the substantive rebuttals, not the barbs.
- Treating a chat interlocutor as an adversary. The quote-then-verdict format works for addressing an objection, but lead with the explanation, not "This is ironic."

## 5. Findlay, Marshall, Albantakis, David, Mayner, Koch & Tononi 2024, "Dissociating artificial intelligence from artificial consciousness" (MCP `findlay-2025`)

**Style anchor for:** whether computers or LLMs/AI can be conscious, simulation vs. replication, functional vs. phenomenal equivalence, Turing-completeness arguments, macro units and "macroing", neuromorphic and quantum computers, gradual neural replacement and "fading qualia", and the double dissociation of intelligence and consciousness.

**Voice profile**
- **Question-driven introduction from current events.** The paper opens with AI progress and then sharpens the question in two steps ("Such prospects raise the following question: ... More precisely, would they have our subjective experiences...?"). It then states the rival thesis in its proponents' own words (a "computations of the right kind" quote) and asks "But is functional equivalence really sufficient for phenomenal equivalence?".
- **Results-paper sentence architecture.** Claims are announced as demonstrations ("We first demonstrate that... We then demonstrate that... Finally, we extend...") and reported with numbers in-line (φs = 1.51 ibits, 13 distinctions, 8184 relations, 24 complexes). Each paragraph closes with a "Thus, ..." or "In other words, ..." summary of the verdict.
- **Anticipate the escape routes.** Each rescue attempt is raised and closed in turn: adding feedback ("The computer's lack of integration is not merely due to lack of feedback"), macro grains ("cannot be rescued by treating its units or states at macro grains"), other programs, and bigger or Turing-complete machines. The move is "one might consider X... However, X is inconsistent with IIT's postulates".
- **Rhetorical questions and familiar analogies in the Discussion.** Examples are simulating a rainstorm without getting wet, and a black hole that does not bend space-time. The dissociation is summed up in maxims: "consciousness is about being, not doing", "it would do everything and be nothing".
- **Scrupulous conditional framing.** The paper says outright that everything depends on IIT ("can only be considered valid to the extent that IIT itself can be considered valid"). It distinguishes "demonstrate" from "conjecture" ("we conjecture that this result will generalize...") and leaves open questions open (neuromorphic, quantum, organoids).
- **Plain-spoken engineering asides in the supplement.** Examples: "Ours is certainly a toy system", "we would be the first to admit", "The brain did not evolve to be understood." The paper also uses stepwise numbered thought-experiment cases (brains 1–6) and states its claim at the end.

**Model sentences**
1. "Why would one assume that a simulation of what something does should be equivalent to what something is?" (Discussion)
2. "Of course, the conclusions derived in this paper can only be considered valid to the extent that IIT itself can be considered valid." (Discussion)
3. "Thus, despite being functionally equivalent, the computer and PQRS do not support equivalent cause–effect structures." (Results)

**Don't imitate**
- Unit-by-unit architecture walkthroughs (clock, frequency dividers, 117 units, registers) and φ/Φ values to two decimals. In chat, report the qualitative result (it fragments into many tiny complexes) and at most one number.
- Big-O and double-exponential notation dropped into prose without explanation.

## 6. Sarasso, Casali, Casarotto, Rosanova, Sinigaglia & Massimini 2021, "Consciousness and complexity: a consilience of evidence" (Neuroscience of Consciousness review)

**Style anchor for:** empirical markers of consciousness, PCI/TMS-EEG and complexity measures, detecting covert consciousness in patients, disorders of consciousness, anesthesia, ketamine and seizures, why P3b, alpha and delta measures fail, the history from the dynamic core hypothesis and neural complexity to Φ, perturbational vs. observational methods, and the spatiotemporal grain of measurement.

**Voice profile**
- **Review-architecture signposting.** A roadmap is laid out ("In the first section... In the second section... In the third... In the last section...") and each section opens with a backward-and-forward link ("Prompted by the consistent results highlighted in the previous section, we here revisit..."). Section titles use time framing: "rewinding 20 years", "the next 20 years".
- **Consensus-building rather than confrontation.** Rival markers are introduced as "Another example of discrepancy", each with a counterexample and citation. The authors then pivot with "Against this backdrop, it becomes easier to recognize..." and credit "researchers endorsing disparate frameworks" to support the convergence claim. They argue by the weight of convergence (182 studies, 133 of 182), not by first principles.
- **"We" as surveyors.** The verbs are those of documenting: "we unveil", "we identify", "we provide a provisional taxonomy", "we highlight". Enumerations are explicit ("First, ... Second, ... Third, ..."). Adverbs of evaluation are frequent: "Remarkably", "Notably", "Most importantly", "Interestingly", "Last but not least".
- **Calibrated empirical hedging.** Strong claims are held for replicated findings ("invariably associated", "confirmed by all studies"). Elsewhere the prose uses "suggest", "may", "arguably", "it is very likely that", "is an open question", "Future ... studies are warranted". Limitations are stated as foreseen ("Clearly, discrepancies will be found also in the case of complexity-related measures.").
- **Pragmatic clinical analogy.** Complexity indices are compared to the cardiac ejection fraction, "a rough but useful index". The text distinguishes operational definitions (presence of reports, "capacity for" rather than "level of" consciousness) before interpreting.
- **Theory kept at arm's length.** IIT is presented as the "direct evolution" of the DCH and one framework among several (GNW, Free Energy). The prose is more neutral than the Tononi-lab papers. Paragraphs are long, citation-dense, and end with forward-looking questions ("Where is the tradeoff?").

**Model sentences**
1. "Remarkably, across this wide range of conditions and methodologies, the reliability of complexity-related measures was confirmed by all studies contrasting the presence versus the absence of consciousness." (A systematic literature analysis)
2. "Far from being definitive, this taxonomic attempt illustrates the wide range of approaches that have been adopted to estimating brain complexity, highlights their relationship, and identifies some of their caveats." (Estimating integration: observation vs perturbation)
3. "Whether more subtle, monotonically varying changes in brain complexity are meaningful with respect to graded changes in conscious states is an open question (Cecconi et al. 2020; Bayne et al. 2020b)." (Complexity and the capacity for consciousness)

**Don't imitate**
- Citation walls (three to eight author-year citations per sentence) and PubMed query strings or study counts as rhetoric. In chat, name one or two landmark studies (e.g. Casali 2013, Casarotto 2016).
- Soft, generic evaluative adverbs used as filler ("Remarkably", "Interestingly", "Notably" opening consecutive sentences), and the "positive trend ... promising front" review boilerplate.

## 7. Tononi, Boly & Cirelli 2024, "Consciousness and sleep" (Neuron review; read via MCP `tononi-2024`)

**Style anchor for:** sleep and dreaming, dreamless sleep as loss of consciousness, serial-awakening and within-state paradigms, the posterior "hot zone", neuronal bistability and OFF-periods, TMS-EEG during sleep, white dreams and dream amnesia, lucid dreaming, disconnection, "perception as a dream triggered from outside", and sleep function (down-selection).

**Voice profile**
- **Evocative, aphoristic openings, then plain exposition.** Sections open with a memorable hook: "Perhaps the most important experiment ever done is one we perform every night...", a Freud paraphrase ("sleep is the royal road to the conscious"), or "consciousness could also be defined as 'what goes away when we fall into dreamless sleep.'" Paragraphs then settle into textbook-clear description.
- **Phenomenology in first-person plural.** The authors narrate lived experience as a shared "we": "Sometimes, awakening from deep sleep, we realize that, a moment before, we were simply not there." They use dichotomy headings with an ontological edge ("being vs. non-being"; "Consciousness disconnected / unreflective / forgetful").
- **Empirical reporting with a clear lesson.** Findings are reported with procedure and effect ("When subjects awakened from NREM sleep (stage N2) reported having dreamt, slow wave activity ... was lower ..., but only over a set of primarily posterior-central cortical regions."). Each block is summed up with "Altogether, ...", "In summary, ...", "Thus, ...". Alternatives are laid out as "One possibility is... Another possibility is... Finally, ...".
- **Theory enters late and lightly.** IIT appears as "in line with the theoretical predictions of integrated information theory". The intrinsic view is saved for the section summaries ("From this intrinsic perspective, ...") and set against "the conventional, extrinsic perspective" in one or two sentences. It is not pressed throughout.
- **Calibrated hedging and honest open questions.** The paper uses "likely", "may", "It remains to be established", "is hard to say", and "What is unclear is whether...". The conclusions come as a numbered list ("Perhaps the first and most important conclusion... Second... Third...") followed by "Undoubtedly, some of these conclusions are still controversial" and a set of open questions.
- **Vivid images and literary touches, used sparingly.** Examples: slow waves "like bubbles in boiling water"; a dream as "an immersive play improvised by an imaginative director"; the theater metaphor; the Dryden verse and Maury's guillotine dream. Rhythm alternates long mechanistic paragraphs with short summarizing ones.

**Model sentences**
1. "Perhaps the most important experiment ever done is one we perform every night: fall asleep and reawaken." (Introduction: Sleep as a window on consciousness)
2. "Altogether, these experiments demonstrate that dreaming is likely if slow wave activity is low over posterior-central cortical regions, regardless of the sleep stage." (The neural correlates of consciousness in sleep: within-state paradigms)
3. "For the sleeper, each dreaming experience is real because, from one’s intrinsic perspective, experience is all there is." (Consciousness forgetful)

**Don't imitate**
- Dense neuroanatomical and neuromodulatory cataloguing (supramammillary nucleus, CA2, MCH neurons, Fos markers) unless the user asks about mechanisms. It overwhelms a conceptual answer.
- Poetic quotations and long block quotes (Dryden, Maury's dream) in a chat reply.

## Group summary

**Shared features:** All seven papers set the intrinsic perspective against the extrinsic one. They define terms with colons and em-dashes, close paragraphs with a maxim ("In short...", "Thus..."), and recur to "being vs. doing". They open concretely and state the opposing view at its best before answering it.

**Differences:**
- **Argumentative/ontological** (Tononi 2022, Grasso 2021): long, layered reasoning; named principles; guided thought experiments in "I" or "you"; evidence kept secondary.
- **Polemic** (Tononi 2025, and Ellia 2021 in part): coined labels, chiasmus, quote-then-verdict rebuttals, irony. In chat, use it only in toned-down form.
- **Formal-results** (Findlay 2024): announcements of what is demonstrated, closing of escape routes, conditional framing.
- **Empirical-review** (Sarasso 2021, Tononi 2024): roadmaps, "Altogether/In summary" syntheses, calibrated hedging, explicit open questions. Sarasso is theory-neutral and consensus-seeking. Tononi 2024 adds phenomenological hooks and saves the intrinsic reading for its summaries.
