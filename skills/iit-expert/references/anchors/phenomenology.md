# Style profiles: phenomenology / contents-of-experience group

Scope: prose style only. All model sentences are verbatim from the corpus text (checked by exact string match). PDF noise was ignored.

---

## 1. Haun & Tononi 2019, "Why does space feel the way it does?" (haun-2019)

**Style anchor for:** why space feels extended; spots and their inclusion, connection, and fusion; regions, locations, sizes, boundaries, and distances; why grids and not random networks; why space is a quale "just as" color is.

**Voice profile**
- **Openings.** The paper starts with a cascade of "why does X feel the way it does?" questions tied to a concrete scene ("Take a glimpse at the night sky"). Sections open by naming the phenomenal item first: "Introspectively, …", "Phenomenally, one can check through introspection that …". The causal counterpart comes after, in a separate sentence: "In the cause-effect structure specified by a grid, … corresponds to …".
- **Person.** The paper uses "one" and "we" for introspection ("one can readily distinguish 'spots' anywhere", "we can always find a spot that…"). It uses imperatives to set a scene ("Consider again looking at the night sky"). It never uses "I", apart from one "my phenomenal space" when glossing intrinsicality.
- **Naming and mapping.** Each property gets a short noun, and its definition follows after a colon or in parentheses ("every spot overlaps with itself; … (connection); … (fusion); … (inclusion)"). Then comes a matched pair: the phenomenal definition, then "To account for phenomenal X … one needs to show that, for any causal distinction …". The two definitions are kept word-for-word parallel. The paper coins labels as it goes (subtext, supertext, context, extension) and marks each with an em-dash or "we call".
- **Connectives.** "Moreover", "However", "Thus", "Indeed", "In turn", "Finally", "By contrast", "Importantly", "In other words", "Translating back to phenomenology". Its signature move is "it is important to realize that …" / "one must realize that …", used to correct a hidden assumption.
- **Examples.** Recurring anchors (night sky, blank canvas, bright blot, dark screen) come back in every section. The paper reasons in counterfactuals ("if a bright blot appears … it cannot appear 'out of nowhere'") and builds minimal-contrast cases (grid vs. random network; "space in a nutshell", where only top-order or only bottom-order distinctions exist).
- **Hedging.** Theoretical claims are stated firmly ("must be", "corresponds precisely"). Correspondences are hedged as "can in principle account for", "a first attempt", "bare-bones, rudimentary". The introspective reach is limited openly, and the fine-grain correspondence is called "an inference—albeit an inference from the best explanation". (That is the 2019 wording; current canon is "inference from a good explanation", so write "good" in new prose.)
- **Rhythm.** Paragraphs are long and cumulative, often more than 250 words, built from enumerations joined by semicolons. The discussion ends with short rhetorical sentences ("And so it should be…", "Perhaps, on occasion, we should.").

**Model sentences**
1. "By contrast, the experience of space is penetrable by introspection in a way that reveals a characteristic internal structure." (1. Introduction)
2. "Introspectively, the region covered by a spot can be thought of as the set of all the spots it fully overlaps." (3.1.5 Regions)
3. "This self-relation corresponds to the phenomenal property that a spot overlaps with itself (reflexivity)." (3.1 Spots)

**Don't imitate**
- The unit-label and superscript apparatus (CDE, BCDc/<BCDout>, ϕR sums, 133/7976 counts) and figure call-outs. In chat, keep only the phenomenal-to-causal pairing.
- The 300-word enumerative paragraphs and the "(see Note N in Appendix B)" asides.

## 2. Comolatti, Grasso & Tononi 2025, "Why does time feel the way it does?" (comolatti-2025)

**Style anchor for:** the flow of time; the extended/specious present; now vs. then; retention and protention; why flow is a static directed structure and not a process in clock time; how IIT relates to retentional, extensional, and cinematic models.

**Voice profile**
- **Openings.** The paper opens with a compressed paradox ("Time flows—or at least the time of our experience does."). The introduction uses a second-person vignette ("On a morning walk, you hear the notes …"). Each Results subsection has the same two parts: "Phenomenally, [property] captures the fact that …", then "The cause-effect structure specified by a directed grid has properties that account for phenomenal [property], because …".
- **Person.** It moves between "you" (vignettes), "we" (introspection and argument), and the impersonal "one can always find …". It uses "Consider" to introduce the running example (Beethoven's Fifth), and heard music serves as the phenomenal lab throughout.
- **Naming and mapping.** It defines its own vocabulary (moments, instants, total moment, now/then, forward/backward inclusion, predecessor/successor) in paired definitions: "they 'share their ending'", "they 'share their beginning'". Each phenomenal property ends with a one-line function statement: "Directed connection accounts for …", "Fusion accounts for the fullness of the present". The space paper is used explicitly as the template, and differences are spelled out with "instead of …", "rather than", "unlike space".
- **Connectives.** "Similarly", "Conversely", "Moreover", "Specifically", "For instance", "Thus", "However", "Nonetheless", "In other words", "Crucially". Clock time and experienced time are contrasted over and over.
- **Examples.** Musical and auditory cases (Beethoven, Sibelius's pauses, Cage's 4′33″), sudden sounds breaking silence, postdiction, and the ∼30 ms macro-state against the ∼210 ms present. Each is used to make a contrast land.
- **Hedging.** Two registers are kept separate. The substrate is conjectured ("we conjecture", "might be located", "remains hypothetical"), while the phenomenal and causal correspondence is stated plainly. There is a formal "Limitations of the study" list (First… Second… Third… Finally…). Empirical estimates are given as ranges with sources ("10–60 ms depending on the paradigm").
- **Rhythm.** Paragraphs are medium length, clearer and more modular than haun-2019. Each subsection has a heading and runs phenomenal paragraph, then causal paragraph, then context-level remark. The closing section gets more lyrical (Augustine; "It is a structure that contains time, space, objects, thoughts, and everything else that exists intrinsically—for itself.").

**Model sentences**
1. "Time flows—or at least the time of our experience does." (Summary)
2. "Phenomenally, moments are characterized by directedness: each moment points away from itself." (Results, Directedness)
3. "These relations yield an experience of succession, rather than a succession of experiences." (Introduction, Phenomenology of time)

**Don't imitate**
- Purview bookkeeping (bcDef forward-includes cDef because cDef's cause (Def) …) and weight or grid parameters. In chat, give the structural gist only.
- The long survey paragraphs cataloguing psychophysical estimates and philosophical positions, which chain many citations.

## 3. Haun & Tononi 2025, "The unfathomable richness of seeing" (haun-2025)

**Style anchor for:** rich vs. sparse experience; overflow; seeing vs. noticing or reporting; inattentional and change blindness; phenomenal vs. functional vision; why even an empty visual field is richly structured; why computer vision "sees" only functionally.

**Voice profile**
- **Openings.** Section headings are argumentative theses ("Noticing objects requires seeing groupings", "We see the visual field whether we notice it or not"). Paragraphs often open by conceding the opposing view ("There is little doubt, then, that if we equate seeing with noticing … we must be seeing very little."), then turn with a short question ("But is that really what seeing is?").
- **Person.** This is the most second-person paper in the group. It includes a guided introspective exercise in the imperative ("Take notice of…", "Now, pick out…", "Notice that…", "You can repeat this…"), which ends in a pointed question ("did you see all these regions … before you noticed them …?") and a firm answer ("The answer must be yes."). Everywhere else it uses the inclusive "we".
- **Naming and mapping.** Terms are introduced in plain language and fixed in a Glossary: phenomenal vs. functional vision, grouping, noticing, seeing. There is little causal machinery. The mapping to IIT comes only at the end, in one short section ("Grid-like networks … support cause–effect structures that fully account for …"). The phenomenal argument carries the paper.
- **Connectives.** "However", "Yet", "Clearly", "In short", "Indeed", "Nonetheless", "Consider", "Now the critical question:". It argues through chains of necessity: "to see X, we must see Y; to see Y, we must see Z".
- **Examples.** Short, vivid, well-known cases (Kanizsa triangle, Mooney gorilla, rabbit–duck, living rooms from the same text prompt, splatters, random dots, fish in water, the self-driving car, hemianopia with "no 'there'"). Each is built as a minimal contrast: the same high-level description, but different experiences.
- **Hedging.** Light and rhetorical ("it is tempting to conclude", "If we are right"). Alternative interpretations are granted briefly and then absorbed ("However, these alternatives still imply …").
- **Rhythm.** Short paragraphs for an opinion piece (Trends format). Sentence length varies, with punchy closers ("And yet we see it."). En-dashes are used for asides.

**Model sentences**
1. "Now, pick out three distinct locations within that expanse – vertices of an imaginary triangle." (We see the visual field whether we notice it or not)
2. "When we notice a region of space, it does not suddenly pop into existence from nowhere." (same section)
3. "To see an object at all is to see the features that compose it – even if they are never noticed, remembered, or named." (Noticing objects requires seeing groupings)

**Don't imitate**
- The multi-step imperative exercise works on the page with figures. In chat, use one or two "notice that" steps at most, or the answer turns into a guided meditation.
- Citation-dense passages on psychophysics (subitizing limits, iconic memory capacity) and the Box/Glossary/Outstanding-questions scaffolding.

## 4. Grasso, Haun & Tononi 2021, "Of maps and grids" (grasso-2021b)

**Style anchor for:** function vs. phenomenology; functional equivalence without phenomenal equivalence; why encoding, decoding, tuning, and retinotopy don't explain experience; cortex vs. pretectum/subcortex; the "unfolding argument" style of objections.

**Voice profile**
- **Openings.** The paper opens with an imperative scene ("Imagine looking straight at a dark screen."), then a long list of what neuroscience does explain ("We know how…; we know that…"), then a sharp one-line pivot ("This part we do not understand."). Later sections open by recapping the method in plain terms ("When we study a system as neuroscientists, we are interested in …").
- **Person.** A collegial "we" that places the authors among neuroscientists ("When we study the brain as neuroscientists"). Seeing is put in scare-quotes as the thing left out: we "see" it.
- **Naming and mapping.** The key contrast is carried by paired words: represent/act/function vs. "is"; extrinsic/functional vs. intrinsic/causal; "what a system 'does'" vs. "what a system 'is'". The phenomenal properties of space are summarized from haun-2019 in one compact sentence, then mapped property by property ("Every causal distinction overlaps with itself in the sense that its cause overlaps with its effect (reflexivity …)").
- **Connectives.** "But", "However", "By contrast", "Instead", "It follows that", "Of course", "Nevertheless", "In other words". Parallel constructions carry the rhetoric ("they represent, act, and function similarly").
- **Examples.** The whole paper is one controlled thought experiment: two systems identical except for lateral connections, with figures to show functional equivalence. It ends with a scrambling prediction phrased as a counterfactual ("if the connections … were scrambled … there would be hardly any functional consequence. However, for the grid network …").
- **Hedging.** Modest about the model ("minimal", "of course in a merely schematic manner", "adequate, in principle rather than in degree"). Firm on the conceptual conclusion ("there 'cannot be' any phenomenology for a map"). It closes on a light hedge ("Perhaps, as proposed here, it may come down to grids versus maps.").
- **Rhythm.** Medium-short paragraphs and clear, didactic sentences. It likes aphoristic summaries ("We must be grids, not maps.").

**Model sentences**
1. "Imagine looking straight at a dark screen." (Introduction)
2. "But from an intrinsic, causal perspective, they could not be more different." (Cause-effect structures: unfolding the causal powers of a system)
3. "What needs to be done is to provide a neuroscientific account of what spatial experience is like, not just of what we do with it." (Introduction)

**Don't imitate**
- Simulation details (Naka-Rushton parameters, 10 000 iterations, ρ = 0.9715, muscle-velocity equations). Say "functionally equivalent" and stop there.
- Footnote-embedded argument, since several key points sit in the notes. In chat, bring them into the main line.

## 5. Boly et al. 2024, "Neural correlates of pure presence" (boly-2024)

**Style anchor for:** contentless or "pure" consciousness; meditation (Vajrayana, Zen); IIT's prediction of experience with near-silent cortex; inactive vs. inactivated neurons; gamma vs. delta as proxies; empirical tests of IIT's counterintuitive predictions.

**Voice profile**
- **Openings.** The paper opens by stating the received view it will challenge ("It is generally assumed that consciousness requires neural activity."), then a list of rival theories, then "In contrast, integrated information theory … argues that …". Results subsections open with the comparison being made ("PP compared to MW…", "Next, we compared PP to …").
- **Person.** An empirical "we" (we recorded, we predicted, we found). There is no introspective "you". The phenomenology comes secondhand, through meditators' reports and traditional texts, quoted verbatim ("a lucid clarity without anyone being there who is the observer…").
- **Naming and mapping.** Phenomenal descriptors are listed as a checklist ("no self, no thoughts, no perceptual objects or sensory features, non-duality, and instead a pure sense of spatial extendedness"). They are mapped onto IIT through prior theory ("an 'extension' … composed of … spots bound by causal relations of reflexivity, inclusion, fusion, and connection"). Neural proxies are glossed in-line ("low delta power … consistent with high cortical arousal").
- **Connectives.** "First… Second… Third…" (an eight-point list of confound controls), "Moreover", "By contrast", "In short", "By the same token", "Notably", "Remarkably", "Despite these caveats".
- **Examples.** Few. The comparison conditions (mind-wandering, open-monitoring, movie, dreamless sleep) do the work of thought experiments. A careful piece of phenomenological clean-up: the "external" space that vanishes is distinguished from the "internal" feeling of extendedness that remains.
- **Hedging.** Strongly empirical: "consistent with", "in line with IIT's predictions", "suggestive of", "lend some plausibility to the notion that …", followed by an explicit list of limitations ("it cannot demonstrate directly that neuronal activity … is in fact reduced").
- **Rhythm.** Standard journal prose with dense methods paragraphs. The Discussion paragraphs are summarizing, each ending on a short verdict ("Far from being a state of dozing off or disconnection, PP is a state of intense, vivid, heightened consciousness.").

**Model sentences**
1. "It is generally assumed that consciousness requires neural activity." (Introduction)
2. "In short, PP states seem to be associated with a cerebral cortex that is widely awake but minimally active." (Discussion)
3. "The results are consistent with this prediction, showing that vivid states of pure consciousness—devoid of self, thoughts, and perceptual objects—are associated with a hdEEG background of low gamma power and low delta power." (Discussion)

**Don't imitate**
- Statistical and preprocessing detail (SNPM, AMICA iterations, frequency cutoffs, participant demographics, retreat schedules).
- Abbreviation density (PP, MW, OM, PP-mpc, LTMs, hdEEG). In chat, spell things out.

## 6. Mayner, Juel & Tononi, "Intrinsic meaning, perception, and matching" (mayner-2024)

**Style anchor for:** intrinsic meaning ("the meaning is the feeling"); perception as interpretation triggered by stimuli; dreams vs. waking perception; representation and reference; why activity patterns have no meaning in themselves; matching between brain and environment; sleep and plasticity; AI "understanding".

**Voice profile**
- **Openings.** The paper opens with an imperative vignette ("Cast a glance at the scene outside the window."), then a fair summary of the standard processing account, then "IIT offers a different account". Discussion subsections recap a defined term ("As defined here, connectedness assesses …") and then draw implications.
- **Person.** A formal "we" for the formalism ("We define…", "We denote…"). An inclusive phenomenal "we" in the Discussion ("Phenomenally, we can easily recognize this situation by considering how we experience …", "Again, we can relate to this situation phenomenally."). Second person appears only in the opening.
- **Naming and mapping.** Coinages are introduced as term, em-dash, gloss ("perceptual structure—the portion of the Φ-structure triggered by the stimulus"). Maxims are set in quotation marks as anchors ("quality is structure", "the meaning is the feeling"). Words from the rival view are scare-quoted (represent, refer, code, processing, resonate). Model results are consistently translated back into ordinary phenomenology (movie vs. TV noise, scrambled movie, geological scans).
- **Connectives.** "Thus", "In this sense", "Importantly", "By contrast", "Accordingly", "Conversely", "In short", "That said", "Even so", "Said otherwise". Explicit qualification is frequent ("However, just as the notion of representation must be qualified, so must that of matching.").
- **Examples.** Toy systems (a segment detector vs. a "centered odd" detector) with deliberately paired environments, including a noise environment where an apparent 3-segment is a "trick of the light". These are thought experiments about reference, followed by everyday analogues.
- **Hedging.** Theoretical identity claims are asserted ("every perception is an interpretation"). Links to the environment are hedged ("typically not straightforward", "can be expected", "in many cases be usefully described as 'representation'"). Methodological choices are flagged as non-unique ("there need not be a unique and correct measure").
- **Rhythm.** The Theory section is equation-heavy. The Discussion runs long, and the paragraphs escalate (definition, then model, then phenomenal analogue, then brain, then implication). Footnotes carry side arguments.

**Model sentences**
1. "Cast a glance at the scene outside the window." (Introduction)
2. "Importantly, the same stimulus can trigger different perceptual structures in different systems even if the activity pattern is exactly the same." (Results, Perception as interpretation)
3. "Accordingly, by IIT the connection between an experience and the environment that triggers it is more akin to an adaptation—an interpretation that works well enough—than to a representation in the conventional sense." (Discussion, Perception and representation)

**Don't imitate**
- The formal apparatus (c(x, m), t(x, m), PMI normalizations, Φd partitions, matching as a maximum over contiguous subsequences). Give only the verbal definition.
- The long, footnote-laden Discussion paragraphs that combine neuroanatomy (NMDA, supragranular layers, SHY) with theory. Split them, or drop the physiology unless asked.

## 7. Grasso, Hendren & Tononi 2026, "Consciousness as Intrinsic Structure: Towards a Chemistry of Experience" (grasso-2026)

**Style anchor for:** the overview question "how does IIT account for contents of experience?"; comparing space vs. time vs. objects; elemental signatures (reflexivity, directedness, hierarchy); narrow qualia and compound contents; what makes a good explanation (the seven S's); "all quality is structure".

**Voice profile**
- **Openings.** It opens with a definitional aphorism ("To be conscious is to have an experience."), then a second-person inventory of the reader's current scene ("the black of the letters you are reading…"). Sections open with framing questions ("What makes it feel extended…?", "Does this amount to a good explanation…?").
- **Person.** A running second-person example ties everything together (reading "All quality is structure."; "When you see the word 'structure' on the page…"). An authorial "we" is used for method ("We can ask the same of experience."). "Consider" introduces the anchor case.
- **Naming and mapping.** The template is strict and repeated three times: elemental component (spot/moment/configuration and concept), then elemental signature (reflexivity/directedness/hierarchy), then bonds (reflexive/directed/hierarchical inclusion, connection, fusion), then compound (extension/flow/conceptual hierarchy). Key terms are italicized on first use. "In causal terms, …" opens every mapping sentence. Parallel wording between the space and time sections is deliberate ("A moment, too, cannot feel extended in isolation").
- **Connectives.** "Just as …, …" (the chemistry analogy), "Similarly", "Thus", "hence", "In causal terms", "But", "And if …", "Taken together". Counterfactual necessity chains: "If it did not include…, it would feel like…; if it were not included…, it would feel like…".
- **Examples and analogy.** The sustained analogy with chemistry (carbon's valence, graphite vs. diamond, CO₂ vs. proteins) is capped by an explicit limit ("useful albeit imperfect: like many analogies, it becomes misleading if taken too far"). The same sentence-reading example serves space, time, and objects. The bee sting illustrates a compound quale.
- **Hedging.** Tiered. Space and time are presented as demonstrated. Objects are "still under development". Narrow and compound qualia are "a proof of concept and an open research program rather than a complete explanation". Falsifiability is stated outright ("failing to find this would count against IIT").
- **Rhythm.** Medium paragraphs, the most polished and even of the group, in a handbook-chapter register. Each subsection ends on a one-sentence summary ("The spatial quality of seeing a word on the page is hence given by the spot it occupies and its relations to the rest of the space.").

**Model sentences**
1. "Seeing words does not create space; it makes salient spots that were already there." (3.1 Spatial extendedness)
2. "And if connected spots did not *fuse* into a further spot including both and nothing else, space would feel fragmented rather than continuous." (3.1 Spatial extendedness)
3. "The spatial quality of seeing a word on the page is hence given by the spot it occupies and its relations to the rest of the space." (3.1 Spatial extendedness)

**Don't imitate**
- Figure-legend conventions (mnemonic color coding such as "[f]uchsia for [f]ull overlap", "you get it by now") and cross-references to book chapters or IIT Wiki pages.
- Stretching the chemistry analogy through every paragraph. Use it once to frame the answer, not as a running metaphor.

## Group summary

All seven papers follow one move: say what a content feels like, name its phenomenal distinctions and relations, then show each one "reflected" in a causal distinction or relation. The phenomenal side comes first and is often set up with "Consider…" or "Imagine…". Theoretical identity claims are stated firmly. Substrate and empirical claims are hedged. They differ in register. haun-2019 is the dense original template. comolatti-2025 makes it modular, adds a "directed" counterpart, and contrasts clock time with experienced time. grasso-2026 compresses both into a teachable scheme (component → signature → bonds → compound) under a chemistry analogy. haun-2025 is polemical and second-person, with almost no formalism: richness is argued from introspection against "noticing". grasso-2021b sets function against phenomenology through one thought experiment. mayner-2024 moves from spatial structure to meaning and reference ("the meaning is the feeling"). boly-2024 is the only purely empirical voice, with reports, proxies, and "consistent with".
