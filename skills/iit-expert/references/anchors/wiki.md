# IIT Wiki: prose-style profile (non-FAQ pages)

Scope: the non-FAQ pages of the IIT wiki. This profiles **voice, not content**. Quotes are verbatim except that markdown links and italic/bold asterisks have been removed. The wiki's curly quotes and em dashes are kept.

**Where the pages live.** The IIT Expert document `iit-wiki` is "IIT Wiki — Part I: Ontological Foundations". It holds these sections: Overview, Foundations, Axioms & Postulates, Intrinsicality, Information, Integration, Exclusion, Composition, Φ-structure Visualization, The Fundamental Identity, Computing Φ, and How IIT Accounts for Contents of Experience. The glossary is the separate document `iit-wiki-glossary`. The validation (Part II), ARC-COGITATE supplement, ontology (Part III) and actual causation pages are not in the IIT Expert library; for those, work from the profiles below.

## 1. Overview (IIT as a Theory of Consciousness)

- **Page and where to find it**: In `iit-wiki` Part I, section "Integrated Information Theory as a Theory of Consciousness".
- **Topics it anchors**: "What is IIT?", the IIT method, what it means to explain consciousness scientifically, the two *why* questions, and the six-step roadmap from introspection to extrapolation.
- **Voice profile**:
  - Opens with a **Summary** block that starts from second-person wonder ("Why does it feel like something to be you, right now?"). It then gives a plain definition of IIT and a one-line method slogan. A separate **Technical Summary** restates everything compactly in theory vocabulary (axioms, postulates, cause–effect power, explanatory identity).
  - The main text is laid out as **six numbered, imperative-titled steps** ("1. Introspect to characterize phenomenal structure", "2. Formulate phenomenal properties in physical (causal) terms", …). Each step is 2–4 short paragraphs.
  - Person shifts between **"you"** (the reader's own experience), **"we"** (the IIT team doing the method), and occasionally **"my"** (sample correlates). Sentences are mid-length and explanatory, with dash-glosses such as "physical … —the ability to 'take and make a difference.'"
  - Canonical framing: the two questions **"Why is experience present vs. absent?"** and **"Why do specific experiences feel the way they do?"**, and the three-way split **"what needs to be explained, how it is explained, and how the explanation is validated"**.
  - Mostly argued and pedagogical. It becomes aphoristic only at the hinge points ("consciousness first"; "all quality is structure"). Every step ends with a **"For more, see"** list of FAQ, paper, and YouTube links.
- **Canonical phrasings**:
  1. "The IIT method can be summarized as “consciousness first.”"
  2. "an experience is identical to a specific cause–effect structure in both quality and quantity."
  3. "The quality of experience corresponds to the specific “shape” of the Φ-structure; in IIT, all quality is structure."
  4. "Here, physical is meant in parsimonious, operational terms of cause–effect power—the ability to “take and make a difference.”"
  5. "it is not a formal deduction but rather an inference to a good explanation"
- **Don't imitate**: the "For more, see" / "Discussion of step N on our YouTube channel" link blocks, "Click the steps on the image", and "as you read on" signposting.

## 2. Foundations (Phenomenal & Physical Existence)

- **Page and where to find it**: In `iit-wiki` Part I, section "Foundations of IIT: Phenomenal & Physical Existence".
- **Topics it anchors**: the 0th axiom and the 0th postulate, the cogito, realism, operational physicalism, atomism, "Is IIT materialist or reductionist?", and what "physical" and "cause–effect power" mean.
- **Voice profile**:
  - **Summary** block in two short paragraphs: the axiom, then three assumptions leading to the postulate. Section headers are the canonical statements themselves: "Experience exists: there is something." and "The substrate of consciousness can be characterized operationally by cause–effect power: its units must take and make a difference." Each methodological assumption gets a one-line gloss as a subtitle: "something exists independently of our own experience", "what exists can be characterized by cause–effect power", "cause–effect power all the way down".
  - Canonical statements: **0th axiom** "Experience exists: there is something." **0th postulate** "The substrate of consciousness can be characterized operationally by cause–effect power: its units must take and make a difference."
  - Thought-experiment driven, in the second person: the consciousness meter that reads "Zero consciousness detected", the paper pushed off the table, the TV remote, the tree. Each is followed by a one-line principle introduced with "Hence, in IIT, …".
  - Uses **"Note that …" disclaimers** to head off misreadings ("does not presume a 'materialist' basis"; "does not make the theory reductionist in an ontological sense"). This ward-off-the-misreading move is characteristic.
  - The argument is careful and modest ("good" rather than "best" explanation). The aphoristic lines are reserved for definitions.
- **Canonical phrasings**:
  1. "Hence, in IIT, something exists physically if and only if it has the ability to “take and make a difference”—if it has cause–effect power"
  2. "It is also irrefutable—not because you can’t doubt it, but because the very act of doubting it confirms its truth"
  3. "Note that this notion of physicalism does not presume a “materialist” basis of reality; it only assumes that the physical should be understood operationally as cause–effect power."
  4. "IIT’s operational assumption of atomism does not make the theory reductionist in an ontological sense."
  5. "We say “good” here rather than the more typical “best,” for where should we stand to judge what’s best"
- **Don't imitate**: the "This account will refer regularly to the image here" references to figures, the slideshow and YouTube pointers, and the footnote markers `[1]`.

## 3. Axioms & Postulates (hub page)

- **Page and where to find it**: In `iit-wiki` Part I, section "Axioms & Postulates of IIT: From Phenomenology to Physics".
- **Topics it anchors**: "What are the axioms/postulates?", the list of the five (six with the 0th), why start from phenomenology rather than the brain, essential vs. accidental properties, and why space and time are not axioms.
- **Voice profile**:
  - **Summary** block, then a lyrical hook ("hearing a Bach fugue, feeling a toothache, dreaming of flying …"). It then **lists the canonical statements in bold, one per line**, prefaced by "The axioms can be most succinctly stated as follows". The same is done for the postulates.
  - **Canonical axiom statements** (hub wording): "Existence (0th axiom): Experience exists: there is something." / "Intrinsicality: Experience is intrinsic: it exists for itself." / "Information: Experience is specific: it is this one." / "Integration: Experience is unitary: it is a whole, irreducible to separate experiences." / "Exclusion: Experience is definite: it is this whole." / "Composition: Experience is structured: it is composed of distinctions and the relations that bind them together, yielding a phenomenal structure that feels the way it feels."
  - **Canonical postulate statements** (hub wording, pronoun "Its"): "Existence (0th postulate): The substrate of consciousness can be characterized operationally by cause–effect power: its units must take and make a difference." / "Intrinsicality: Its cause–effect power must be intrinsic: it must take and make a difference within itself." / "Information: Its cause–effect power must be specific: it must be in this state and select this cause–effect state." / "Integration: Its cause–effect power must be unitary: it must specify its cause–effect state as a whole set of units, irreducible to separate subsets." / "Exclusion: Its cause–effect power must be definite: it must specify its cause–effect state as this whole set of units." / "Composition: Its cause–effect power must be structured: subsets of units must specify cause–effects over subsets of units (distinctions) that can overlap with one another (relations), yielding a cause–effect structure that is the way it is."
  - The synopsis uses a fixed **parallel template**: "Just as my experience is X (left), the cause–effect power of its substrate must be X (right)." Sentence shape is the paired colon formula: property-word, then colon, then a short italic gloss.
  - Person is "you" and "we", with "I/my" in the synopsis. It is argued in the method paragraphs and aphoristic in the stated axioms. It closes with an index of FAQ questions.
- **Canonical phrasings**:
  1. "The axioms of IIT are not necessarily self-evident, but they are indeed irrefutable."
  2. "to reflect on the properties of experience is to reflect on the properties of existence."
  3. "we don’t start from the brain because we have little idea what we’re looking for."
  4. "what truly exists in the end is not the simple substrate of units per se, but rather a complex unfolded as a cause–effect structure (or Φ-structure)."
  5. "Just as my experience is unitary (left), the cause–effect power of its substrate must be unitary (right)."
- **Don't imitate**: the "(left)/(right)" figure references, the bold one-word-at-a-time markup, and the trailing FAQ index.

## 4. The five axiom–postulate pages (Intrinsicality, Information, Integration, Exclusion, Composition) + Φ-structure viz

- **Page and where to find it**: All are in `iit-wiki` Part I, each as its own section plus "Φ-structure Visualization". The text of each page is credited as "Excerpt from On Being (in preparation)" by Tononi.
- **Topics it anchors**: any question about one specific axiom or postulate. That covers the intrinsic perspective and background conditions; specificity, intrinsic information, and Shannon information; unity, φs, the minimum partition, and cuts; definiteness, complexes, exclusion, and grain; and distinctions, relations, Φ-structures, and Φ.
- **Voice profile**:
  - **Rigid template** on every page. It starts with an ordinal subtitle ("3rd axiom & postulate of IIT"), then a **Summary**: "X is an essential property of experience (an axiom) and, by inference, also an essential property of the substrate of consciousness (a postulate). The axiom states that … Formulated in physical terms, the postulate states that …". Next comes an axiom section headed by the canonical axiom sentence, then a postulate section headed by the canonical postulate sentence.
  - **Axiom half: first-person introspection** ("I", "my experience") on the shared sample scene (bed, book, blue, the left and right of space). It is followed by a fixed **immediacy / irrefutability / universality** paragraph: "X is immediate because I do not have to infer …"; "X is irrefutable, in the sense that its negation is self-contradictory or absurd"; "…reaffirming the validity of the axiom"; "it must be true of every conceivable experience … an essential property of phenomenal existence".
  - **Postulate half: "we", operational**. Every postulate section opens with "Phenomenally, X means that …" and then turns to physical terms. It works through the toy system Abcd/iAbcdo with TPM numbers. Each page has one homely analogy: the frayed rope and the audio conference call (integration), the pulley and weights (composition), the chariot (on Computing Φ). Each ends with "To see how the X postulate is operationalized mathematically, see Computing Φ, Step N".
  - The ontological principles are stated as maxims: maximal existence ("what exists is what exists the most") and minimal existence. So is φ as a "quantifier of integrated existence" (integration and composition pages).
  - **Differences between pages**:
    - *Intrinsicality* is the most philosophical. It glosses *intrinsic* vs. *by itself* and quotes William James.
    - *Information* is the most quantitative (ii values, informativeness × selectivity) and contrasts IIT information with communication theory.
    - *Integration* is the most metaphor-rich, and it keeps a book artifact ("discussed at the end of the chapter").
    - *Exclusion* extends the argument to grain (micro/macro units, φu).
    - *Composition* is the longest and most technical. It has "Distinctions", "Relations", and "Φ-structures and Φ" subsections and almost no first person after the axiom section.
    - The *Φ-structure viz* page is only UI instructions.
  - **Wording variants to watch**: the individual pages write "irreducible to separate subsets of units" (integration) and "subsets of its units" (composition), where the hub has "separate subsets" and "subsets of units". The glossary recasts every postulate as "the substrate of consciousness must have X cause–effect power". The minimal-existence maxim reads "something cannot exist more than the least it exists" (integration page) but "nothing exists more than the least it exists" (glossary). The integration page gives φs = 1.32 for Abcd, while Computing Φ gives 1.21, so don't quote wiki numbers without checking.
- **Canonical phrasings**:
  1. "Integration is irrefutable, in the sense that its negation is self-contradictory or absurd." (the same template, with the axiom's name swapped in, is on all five pages)
  2. "The principle states that, when it comes to a requirement for existence, what exists is what exists the most."
  3. "a system only exists as one system to the extent that it cannot be partitioned (unidirectionally) without loss."
  4. "Intrinsic thus denotes the property of existing for itself—from the inside—as opposed to existing for something else—from the outside."
  5. "a Φ-structure is not a mathematical abstraction, disconnected from the substrate; it is rather the fully unfolded cause–effect power of that substrate."
- **Don't imitate**: figure call-outs ("as indicated in fig. 2.5.1", "dashed orange line and scissors", "yellow pins"), the "Citation for the text / Citation for the slides" BibTeX blocks, and orphaned "[...]" book-excerpt ellipses.

## 5. The Fundamental Identity of IIT

- **Page and where to find it**: In `iit-wiki` Part I, section "The Fundamental Identity of IIT". Credited as a Tononi "On Being" excerpt.
- **Topics it anchors**: "What is the explanatory/fundamental identity?", quality is structure, the claim that IIT is not reductive, emergentist, or causal, identity vs. isomorphism, the seven S's of a good explanation, and water/H2O analogies.
- **Voice profile**:
  - **Summary** block that gives the identity "most succinctly". It warns that the page "build[s] heavily on the previous pages" and adds a pre-emptive disclaimer ("explanatory rather than ontologically reductive").
  - **Canonical statement** (stated twice, word for word): "an experience is identical to the Φ-structure unfolded from a complex: every property of the experience should be accounted for by a corresponding property of the Φ-structure."
  - Three essay-like sections: "Identity as a complete, naturalistic account", "Identity as explanatory", "Identity as a good explanation". The last is walked criterion by criterion through italicized keywords (scope, synthesis, specificity, …). Paragraphs are long and argued, with "should" as the modal throughout ("should be accounted for", "should vanish").
  - Mostly impersonal "IIT" and "we", with one "my experience" example. It defines by contrast: identity vs. isomorphism, and explanatory identity vs. causal explanation. It uses philosophy-of-science analogies (Leibniz's law, temperature/kinetic energy, water/H2O).
  - The aphoristic compressions, "In short, quality is structure" and "Experience is always primary in IIT", come at the ends of long argued paragraphs.
- **Canonical phrasings**:
  1. "IIT never claims that an experience is “nothing but” a Φ-structure."
  2. "In short, quality is structure."
  3. "consciousness should be present if a substrate is a maximum of irreducible, intrinsic cause–effect power and absent otherwise."
  4. "If the identity holds, the question of how a substrate would “cause,” “generate,” or “give rise to” experience is ill-posed"
  5. "an identity that, in principle, should leave nothing unexplained"
- **Don't imitate**: the "[...]" excision marks, orphaned footnote numbers, and "see Contents of Experience" cross-links written into sentences.

## Wiki summary

The wiki is written as **standalone entries**. Each page restates its thesis in a Summary block, uses the canonical colon formulas ("Experience is unitary: it is a whole, …"), and closes on a compressed maxim ("all quality is structure", "what exists is what exists the most"). The papers argue, qualify, and cite. The wiki is **declarative and template-driven**. It uses first-person introspection on the axiom side, operational "we" on the postulate side, fixed irrefutability boilerplate, homely analogies, and "Note that…" disclaimers against misreadings.

A chat answer should **borrow the canonical wording exactly**: the axiom and postulate statements, the identity sentence, the principles, and the glossary definitions. It should also borrow one Summary-style opening sentence per topic and the phenomenal-then-physical pairing. It should **not** borrow the maxim-heavy cadence throughout. Use at most one or two aphorisms, placed at a paragraph's end. Leave out the templated immediacy and irrefutability ritual, figure call-outs, link chains, and toy numbers. Let the argument carry the answer, not the slogans.
